AI Evaluation & Safety Researcher · Founder & Director, Taraaz

CV GitHub LinkedIn Newsletter Email

Roya Pakzad is an AI evaluation and safety researcher and the founder and director of Taraaz, an independent research organization working on AI safety evaluation, trust and safety, and human rights. With a background in electrical engineering and human rights studies, she has spent more than a decade across engineering, trust and safety, and technology policy. Her current focus is whether evaluations and guardrails stay reliable across languages, product contexts, and agentic systems.

She works with policy and domain experts to turn safety concerns into scenarios, behavioral policies and rubrics, human-review protocols, and LLM-judge pipelines. She has red-teamed models for OpenAI (GPT-4) and Meta, and has led evaluation and risk engagements for OpenAI, Google, Meta, and the Wikimedia Foundation.

In 2026 she was a Builder-in-Residence at Mozilla.ai, and from 2025 to early 2026 a Senior Fellow in Trustworthy AI at the Mozilla Foundation, where she built the Multilingual AI Safety Evaluation Lab. Earlier, she was a Data & Democracy Fellow at the University of Virginia, a research associate and project lead at Stanford's Global Digital Policy Incubator, and an electrical engineer at AMD, where she worked on the custom chips for PlayStation 4 and Xbox One. She holds an M.A. in Human Rights Studies from Columbia University, an M.Sc. in Electrical Engineering from USC, and a B.Sc. in Electrical Engineering from Shahid Beheshti University.

Her work has been featured in Wired, the Financial Times, BBC, CNN, and Reuters, and has been recognized by the Center for Applied Data Ethics at USF, the Rockefeller Foundation Bellagio Center, Meta, and Twitter Engineering. She was born and raised in Tehran, Iran, and lives in Santa Cruz, California.

Research

I build evaluation methods for multilingual harms, content moderation, generative AI, and agentic systems, turning written safety policies into criteria that people and LLM judges can grade reliably and into recommendations that product and policy teams can act on.

Highlights

Guardrail Agentic

An open-source framework that converts a written safety policy into structured behavioral criteria and grading guidance for agentic and non-agentic LLM judges, using web search and retrieval for evidence-based verification. Tested on tool-using systems in humanitarian assistance, finance, and cybersecurity, comparing generic and context-specific policies across languages. (FAccT'26 Tutorial, NeurIPS'26 AI for Good Workshop)

Multilingual AI Safety Evaluation Lab

An open-source, human-in-the-loop framework for scenario-based evaluation, structured grading, and cross-model comparison. With Respond Crisis Translation, 8 language experts produced 655 human evaluations of GPT, Gemini, and Mistral across English, Farsi, Arabic, Kurdish Sorani, and Pashto, showing large drops in actionability outside English. (GitHub, Mozilla Data Collective dataset)

Multilingual, Context-Aware Guardrails

With Mozilla.ai, an evaluation of three guardrail systems (FlowJudge, Glider, and an AnyLLM-based guardrail) against human annotation on 60 asylum-seeker scenarios, testing whether scores hold when the policy language changes. (Blog'26)

Bilingual Shadow Reasoning

A red-teaming framework, submitted to OpenAI's GPT-OSS-20B Red Teaming Challenge, testing how customized deliberative-reasoning policies and policy language steer model behavior, including hidden cross-language steering and divergence between reasoning and output. (2025)

Multilingual Policy Testing for gpt-oss-safeguard

An OpenAI, ROOST, and Hugging Face hackathon project testing an open, policy-following safety classifier across languages; included in the ROOST model-community repository. (Dec 2025)

Red-Teaming Frontier Models

External red-teamer for OpenAI (GPT-4, English and Farsi, including visual inputs) and Meta, reporting failures to the developers' safety teams. Advisor to the DEF CON 31 Generative AI Red-Team Challenge. (GPT-4 System Card)

U.S. Responsible AI Procurement Index & Government AI Oversight

Built from scratch an index assessing federal AI procurement and public accountability across 15 departments and 22 measurable indicators, and developed oversight frameworks that tie testing, documentation, and human oversight to vendor accountability for U.S. local governments. (Report'25, IEEE P3119)

Human Rights Impact Assessments

Risk and evaluation engagements for OpenAI, Google, Meta, the Wikimedia Foundation, and civil-society organizations, combining technical testing, policy analysis, and stakeholder interviews. (Wikimedia HRIA'25)
